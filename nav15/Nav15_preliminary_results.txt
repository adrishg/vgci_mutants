Nav1.5 preliminary results

The current Nav1.5 masks do not globally broaden the predicted distance ensemble. For WT masked v2, the
median masked-to-vanilla standard-deviation ratio is 0.94, and 39% of shared distances become more
variable. Excluding the IFM residues from the masked region, and therefore retaining their MSA
information in WT masked-v2-noIFM, gives a similar ratio of 0.95.

The QQQ ensembles show the same global tendency. The standard-deviation ratio is 0.89 for the original
mask and 0.86 for masked v2. Voltage-sensor distributions narrow consistently. The original QQQ mask is
the principal exception, with localized broadening and shifts at the intracellular gate; this response
is reduced in masked v2.

Masking should therefore not be described as universally diversity enhancing. In Nav1.5, its effect is
region and mask-design dependent. RMSD clustering and focused IFM-receptor contact analysis are needed
to determine whether narrower marginal distributions still contain distinct coordinated states.

Gate openness and coordinated IFM-latch sampling

In vanilla predictions, IFM-to-QQQ replacement produces a clear bias toward disengagement of the
inactivation motif. The mean of the two latch distances shifts from 11.92 Å in WT to 28.99 Å in QQQ,
whereas the median maximum intracellular-gate span increases more modestly from 15.76 to 16.03 Å.
The gate-latch correlation within vanilla QQQ is weak (Spearman rho = 0.13), indicating that the strong
latch displacement is not tightly coupled to progressive gate opening across individual models.

Under masked v2 prediction, both WT and QQQ occupy the long-distance latch regime (29.38 and 29.13 Å,
respectively). The masked QQQ gate is nevertheless larger than masked WT (16.81 versus 16.07 Å) and
more variable. Gate-latch coordination is moderate in both masked ensembles (rho = 0.36 for WT and
0.31 for QQQ). Masking therefore promotes a coordinated disengaged-latch/gate ensemble, but the
disengaged latch is no longer QQQ-specific because masked WT undergoes the same large shift.

The mutation-specific state bias is consequently clearest in vanilla: QQQ strongly favors an
unlatched geometry relative to WT. In masked v2, the larger gate sampled by QQQ may represent an
additional mutant effect, but the latch-state shift itself is predominantly a masking effect shared
with WT. These geometric assignments remain preliminary and should not be equated directly with a
functional open or inactivated state until RMSD clustering and structural inspection confirm the
corresponding global conformations.

IFM masking and residual MSA information

The masking labels describe different MSA-masking profiles, not different Nav1.5 sequences.
WT masked v2 and WT masked-v2-noIFM both retain the native IFM amino-acid sequence. In the noIFM
control, the IFM residues are excluded from the masked region, so evolutionary information at the
IFM itself is retained. The QQQ datasets retain the QQQ sequence, while vanilla, masked, and masked-v2
conditions differ in how much MSA information remains around the latch region.

Using the descriptive two-contact classification, WT vanilla contains a substantial partially engaged
population: 57.4% of models have one IFM contact at or below 12 Å, 42.5% have both contacts above
12 Å, and 0.1% satisfy the strict two-contact latch-like definition. QQQ vanilla still retains a small
partially engaged population of 5.3%, despite 94.7% being fully disengaged. This residual population is
consistent with the possibility that unmasked evolutionary information biases the model toward the
canonical latch pocket even when QQQ cannot reproduce the native hydrophobic interaction.

The WT noIFM control does not, however, rescue latch engagement under the present analysis. WT masked
v2 is 96.7% fully disengaged and WT masked-v2-noIFM is 97.7% fully disengaged; their partially engaged
fractions are 3.3% and 2.3%, respectively. Retaining IFM-site MSA information alone is therefore
insufficient to recover the WT-vanilla latch distribution. This may mean that evolutionary constraints
elsewhere in the receptor pocket or surrounding domains are required for latching, that the broader v2
mask dominates the local IFM signal, or that the two chosen Cα contacts do not capture every valid
latch geometry. The current data support residual MSA-driven placement in QQQ vanilla as a hypothesis,
but they do not yet establish it mechanistically.

Voltage-sensor bias relative to WT

IFM-to-QQQ replacement does not produce a uniform VSD contraction. Across the eight shared VSD
landmarks, the median mutant-minus-WT shift is -0.005 Å in vanilla and -0.010 Å in masked v2. Four
contacts become shorter and four become longer in vanilla; masked v2 contains four shorter, two
longer, and two essentially unchanged contacts. The distributions also retain nearly identical
interquartile ranges to WT.

The largest vanilla effects are opposing changes within domain III: F934-R984 becomes shorter by
0.15 Å, whereas F934-K1000 becomes longer by 0.34 Å. Under masked v2 every median VSD shift is within
0.05 Å of WT. The QQQ mutation therefore strongly biases IFM-latch engagement but has little consistent
effect on the VSD landmarks. Masking further suppresses the pair-specific vanilla differences rather
than revealing a closer global voltage-sensor state.

Shortest heavy-atom latch distances

The shortest-distance analysis independently supports the separation between WT vanilla and all
other Nav1.5 ensembles. In WT vanilla, the median shortest distances from the central F1486-equivalent
residue to N1659 and N1765 are 10.0 Å and 13.8 Å, and the shortest distance from any IFM residue to
N1659 is 9.6 Å. In WT masked v2, these medians increase to 28.1, 32.0, and 22.4 Å. Retaining IFM-site
MSA information in WT masked-v2-noIFM gives nearly the same values (27.9, 31.4, and 22.2 Å), confirming
that this control does not restore the WT-vanilla motif placement.

QQQ vanilla and both QQQ masks occupy the same long-distance heavy-atom regime. Their median central
motif-to-N1659 distances range from 27.2 to 27.9 Å, central motif-to-N1765 distances from 31.1 to
31.8 Å, and whole-motif-to-N1659 distances from 22.0 to 22.5 Å. Thus, the shortest-distance result is
not driven by the choice of Cα atoms and strengthens the conclusion that QQQ and masked WT displace
the motif away from the tested receptor coordinates.

Importantly, essentially none of the ensembles, including WT vanilla, place these tested heavy-atom
pairs within 4 Å. The Cα-based “partially engaged” WT-vanilla population should therefore be
interpreted as a closer backbone placement toward the latch region, not as evidence of a resolved
direct side-chain contact to N1659 or N1765. This also explains why the current two-coordinate
classification identifies very few strictly latch-like models despite the clear relative separation
of WT vanilla. The most robust present claim is comparative: WT vanilla uniquely favors a
latch-proximal motif geometry, whereas QQQ mutation or v2 masking shifts both backbone and heavy-atom
coordinates into a disengaged regime. Identification of the actual closest receptor-side partners is
still needed before assigning a specific physical latch interaction.

Expanded experimental-state comparison

The IFM coordinate reference set now includes the full-length human open structures 8VYJ and 8VYK,
human E1784K intermediate-inactivated structure 7DTC, rat BTX-B-stabilized open comparator 8T6L, and
rat reference structure 6UZ3. Their central IFM-to-N1659 and IFM-to-N1765 Cα distances are,
respectively: 8VYJ, 8.037 and 9.896 Å; 8VYK, 7.782 and 10.316 Å; 7DTC, 6.743 and 9.072 Å; 8T6L,
6.764 and 8.585 Å; and 6UZ3, 7.028 and 9.304 Å. Human structures use canonical F1486/N1659/N1765
numbering, whereas the rat structures use the sequence-verified +2 correspondence
F1488/N1661/N1767.

All five calculable structures remain within the descriptive two-contact latch-proximal region despite
spanning native open, intermediate-inactivated, and toxin-stabilized open conditions. This is an
important interpretive constraint: displacement of the IFM motif is not required for experimental
pore opening, and the long-distance masked WT population should not be labeled an open state on the
basis of IFM disengagement alone. Instead, the new full-length structures support a model in which the
IFM motif can be repositioned while remaining receptor proximal, with pore opening, VSD activation,
and CTD–III/IV-linker coupling providing additional state coordinates.

The rat 7FBS QQQ structure cannot be placed on the same IFM-coordinate plot. The engineered
III–IV-linker/QQQ motif positions are not modeled in the deposited coordinates, so neither IFM1 nor
IFM2 can be calculated without fabrication. 7FBS remains a valuable pore-open reference for gate and
S6 distances, but not a direct experimental QQQ-latch point.

RMSF redistribution relative to the targeted masks

These values were recomputed from the final allOK3 ensembles and supersede the legacy all-model RMSF
profiles for quantitative interpretation. The analysis includes 34,998 accepted model snapshots:
4,999 QQQ standard masked, 5,000 QQQ masked v2, 5,000 QQQ vanilla, 5,000 WT standard masked, 4,999
WT masked v2, 5,000 WT masked v2-noIFM, and 5,000 WT vanilla.

Nav1.5 shows a modest but reproducible redistribution beyond the directly masked residues. Under the
standard mask, median masked-minus-vanilla RMSF is +0.056 Å inside the WT mask and +0.031 Å outside
it; for QQQ the corresponding values are +0.055 and +0.042 Å. The v2 profiles have smaller distal
effects: +0.048 Å inside and +0.008 Å outside for WT, and +0.042 and +0.006 Å for QQQ. WT v2-noIFM
shows +0.056 Å inside its direct mask and only +0.004 Å outside. Thus, the standard mask produces the
strongest channel-wide redistribution, whereas v2 confines most of the increase more closely to the
targeted regions.

The provisional segment analysis shows that this response is selective rather than a uniform
increase. DI and portions of DII generally gain variability under the standard mask, while other
segments change little or decrease. The IFM motif remains the strongest local decrease; for WT
standard masking its median masked-minus-vanilla RMSF is -7.71 Å. The masked ensemble therefore
places the motif in a less variable but strongly displaced long-distance regime. Retaining IFM-site
MSA information in the v2-noIFM protocol does not by itself restore the vanilla latch position.

These findings support redistribution of conformational sampling beyond the exact mask boundaries,
but they do not establish directional propagation from the mask to a downstream segment. The similar
DI and DII responses across WT and QQQ indicate a substantial protocol-wide component. The IFM result
is more specific and biologically relevant: masking can reduce the spread of the motif while shifting
its entire ensemble away from the experimentally latch-proximal region. RMSD clustering and
three-dimensional coupling analyses are needed to distinguish coherent state changes from alignment
or coverage effects.

Reconciling QQQ placement with reduced masked RMSF

The direction of an RMSF change should not be equated with functional improvement. In Nav1.5, masking
often lowers RMSF at the IFM/QQQ motif even though the motif is farther from the experimental latch
pocket. The masked ensembles are therefore not sampling a narrower distribution around the native
bound geometry; they are sampling a narrower distribution around a displaced, disengaged geometry.
Reduced RMSF in this case reflects more consistent misplacement rather than stronger latching.

QQQ vanilla provides a useful contrast. Although 94.7% of its models are fully disengaged by the
descriptive two-contact classification, 5.3% retain one latch-proximal Cα coordinate. A plausible
interpretation is that the intact MSA still contains evolutionary information associated with placing
the III-IV linker near the canonical IFM receptor pocket. AlphaFold can partially reproduce that
learned backbone placement even after IFM is replaced by QQQ. This would represent an evolutionary
prior for where the motif normally belongs, not evidence that glutamine residues recreate the native
hydrophobic latch.

The heavy-atom analysis limits the strength of this claim. QQQ vanilla remains in the same
long-distance regime as masked QQQ, with no recurrent direct contact to the tested N1659/N1765
coordinates. Thus, the present data are consistent with residual MSA information weakly biasing a
small fraction of QQQ vanilla structures toward the latch region, but not with recovery of physical
QQQ binding. Masking removes or redistributes that placement signal and produces a more consistently
disengaged motif. This distinction explains why masking can reduce local RMSF while simultaneously
moving the ensemble farther from experimentally latch-proximal Nav1.5 structures.
