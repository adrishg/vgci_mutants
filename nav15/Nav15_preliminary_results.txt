Nav1.5 preliminary results

The current Nav1.5 masks do not globally broaden the predicted distance ensemble. For WT masked v2, the
median masked-to-vanilla standard-deviation ratio is 0.94, and 39% of shared distances become more
variable. Excluding the IFM residues from the masked region, and therefore retaining their MSA
information in WT masked-v2-noIFM, gives a similar ratio of 0.95.

The QQQ ensembles show the same global tendency. The standard-deviation ratio is 0.89 for the original
mask and 0.86 for masked v2. Voltage-sensor distributions narrow consistently. The original QQQ mask is
the principal exception, with localized broadening and shifts at the intracellular gate; this response
is reduced in masked v2.

Masking should therefore not be described as universally diversity enhancing. In Nav1.5, its effect is
region and mask-design dependent. RMSD clustering and focused IFM-receptor contact analysis are needed
to determine whether narrower marginal distributions still contain distinct coordinated states.

Gate openness and coordinated IFM-latch sampling

In vanilla predictions, IFM-to-QQQ replacement produces a clear bias toward disengagement of the
inactivation motif. The mean of the two latch distances shifts from 11.92 Å in WT to 28.99 Å in QQQ,
whereas the median maximum intracellular-gate span increases more modestly from 15.76 to 16.03 Å.
The gate-latch correlation within vanilla QQQ is weak (Spearman rho = 0.13), indicating that the strong
latch displacement is not tightly coupled to progressive gate opening across individual models.

Under masked v2 prediction, both WT and QQQ occupy the long-distance latch regime (29.38 and 29.13 Å,
respectively). The masked QQQ gate is nevertheless larger than masked WT (16.81 versus 16.07 Å) and
more variable. Gate-latch coordination is moderate in both masked ensembles (rho = 0.36 for WT and
0.31 for QQQ). Masking therefore promotes a coordinated disengaged-latch/gate ensemble, but the
disengaged latch is no longer QQQ-specific because masked WT undergoes the same large shift.

The mutation-specific state bias is consequently clearest in vanilla: QQQ strongly favors an
unlatched geometry relative to WT. In masked v2, the larger gate sampled by QQQ may represent an
additional mutant effect, but the latch-state shift itself is predominantly a masking effect shared
with WT. These geometric assignments remain preliminary and should not be equated directly with a
functional open or inactivated state until RMSD clustering and structural inspection confirm the
corresponding global conformations.

IFM masking and residual MSA information

The masking labels describe different MSA-masking profiles, not different Nav1.5 sequences.
WT masked v2 and WT masked-v2-noIFM both retain the native IFM amino-acid sequence. In the noIFM
control, the IFM residues are excluded from the masked region, so evolutionary information at the
IFM itself is retained. The QQQ datasets retain the QQQ sequence, while vanilla, masked, and masked-v2
conditions differ in how much MSA information remains around the latch region.

Using the descriptive two-contact classification, WT vanilla contains a substantial partially engaged
population: 57.4% of models have one IFM contact at or below 12 Å, 42.5% have both contacts above
12 Å, and 0.1% satisfy the strict two-contact latch-like definition. QQQ vanilla still retains a small
partially engaged population of 5.3%, despite 94.7% being fully disengaged. This residual population is
consistent with the possibility that unmasked evolutionary information biases the model toward the
canonical latch pocket even when QQQ cannot reproduce the native hydrophobic interaction.

The WT noIFM control does not, however, rescue latch engagement under the present analysis. WT masked
v2 is 96.7% fully disengaged and WT masked-v2-noIFM is 97.7% fully disengaged; their partially engaged
fractions are 3.3% and 2.3%, respectively. Retaining IFM-site MSA information alone is therefore
insufficient to recover the WT-vanilla latch distribution. This may mean that evolutionary constraints
elsewhere in the receptor pocket or surrounding domains are required for latching, that the broader v2
mask dominates the local IFM signal, or that the two chosen Cα contacts do not capture every valid
latch geometry. The current data support residual MSA-driven placement in QQQ vanilla as a hypothesis,
but they do not yet establish it mechanistically.

Voltage-sensor bias relative to WT

IFM-to-QQQ replacement does not produce a uniform VSD contraction. Across the eight shared VSD
landmarks, the median mutant-minus-WT shift is -0.005 Å in vanilla and -0.010 Å in masked v2. Four
contacts become shorter and four become longer in vanilla; masked v2 contains four shorter, two
longer, and two essentially unchanged contacts. The distributions also retain nearly identical
interquartile ranges to WT.

The largest vanilla effects are opposing changes within domain III: F934-R984 becomes shorter by
0.15 Å, whereas F934-K1000 becomes longer by 0.34 Å. Under masked v2 every median VSD shift is within
0.05 Å of WT. The QQQ mutation therefore strongly biases IFM-latch engagement but has little consistent
effect on the VSD landmarks. Masking further suppresses the pair-specific vanilla differences rather
than revealing a closer global voltage-sensor state.
