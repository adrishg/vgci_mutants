Cav1.2 preliminary results

Masking produces a modest, region-selective expansion of the Cav1.2 distance ensemble. Across shared
observables, the median masked-to-vanilla standard-deviation ratio is 1.05 for WT, 1.09 for G402S, and
1.08 for G406R. Variability increases for 56%, 61%, and 57% of shared distances, respectively.

The effect is strongest in mobile gating regions. Voltage-sensor distances show median
standard-deviation ratios of 1.22 to 1.29. Mutant intracellular-gate interquartile ranges increase by
approximately 42% for G402S and 40% for G406R. Selectivity-filter distances remain comparatively
constrained, with standard-deviation ratios below one.

These findings suggest that masking relaxes selected voltage-sensor and gate geometries without broadly
disrupting the pore. The increase is reproducible but smaller than in Kv2.1. RMSD clustering is still
needed to determine whether the broader distributions represent distinct conformational states.

Gate openness and coordinated state sampling

The joint gate-state analysis does not show a large mutation-dependent bias toward a more open
intracellular gate. Relative to protocol-matched WT, the median maximum gate span changes by only
+0.06 Å for G402S and -0.07 Å for G406R under vanilla prediction, and by +0.12 Å and +0.08 Å,
respectively, under masking. Masking therefore broadens gate sampling more than it displaces the
median gate geometry.

G402S shifts the L401-D1533 mutation-pocket coordinate toward larger distances in both vanilla
(+0.24 Å relative to WT) and masked (+0.35 Å) ensembles. Gate opening and this pocket distance are
negatively correlated in both backgrounds, including G402S itself (Spearman rho = -0.37 in vanilla
and -0.52 when masked). The sampled motion is coordinated, but it does not describe simultaneous
expansion of both coordinates and should not yet be labeled an open-state transition.

G406R also produces a mutation-site bias in both protocols: the E407-R1532 distance is +1.36 Å above
WT in vanilla and +0.95 Å above WT when masked. This local shift is therefore not created by masking.
Masking broadens the gate and strengthens a negative gate-interface relationship (rho = -0.44 in
masked G406R, compared with +0.28 in vanilla G406R). Both Cav1.2 mutants alter their local S6-interface
geometry in vanilla and masked predictions, whereas evidence for a mutation-specific bias toward a
globally more open pore remains weak.

Voltage-sensor bias relative to WT

Neither Cav1.2 mutant shows a consistent VSD-wide contraction relative to WT. For G402S, the median
mutant-minus-WT shift across the eight VSD distances is -0.005 Å in vanilla and +0.010 Å when masked.
Four of eight contacts are shorter in vanilla, whereas only two are shorter under masking. The largest
individual response is localized to domain III: F940-K1015 becomes shorter (-0.08 to -0.09 Å), while
F940-K1031 becomes longer (+0.08 Å in vanilla and +0.21 Å when masked). This is a pair-specific
rearrangement rather than uniform VSD compaction.

G406R is similarly close to WT, with median shifts of +0.015 Å in vanilla and +0.010 Å when masked.
Most vanilla contacts are slightly longer, whereas the masked ensemble contains three shorter and five
longer contacts. All median shifts remain within 0.08 Å. VSD spread is also similar to or slightly
narrower than WT. Thus, the Cav1.2 mutations alter the local S6 interface much more clearly than they
bias the VSDs, and masking does not uncover a common closer-VSD state.

Shortest-distance contacts of the introduced residues

G402S produces a modest, partner-specific contact change rather than a stable new interaction network.
The clearest candidate is S402-I1523: 19.2% of vanilla and 12.1% of masked mutant models place the two
residues within 4 Å, compared with no corresponding G402-I1523 contacts in WT. The median shortest
distance decreases from 6.5 Å in WT to 4.8 Å in G402S under both protocols. S402-D1528 contacts are
rare (1.0% in vanilla and 0.1% when masked), and the other tested partners do not form recurrent
contacts. Conversely, the frequent WT G402-M1524 proximity is lost: the WT contact fraction is 31.1%
in vanilla and 58.0% when masked, compared with 0.0% and 1.6% for S402. Masking therefore reduces the
new S402-I1523 population and does not strengthen a unique G402S contact.

G406R has a much stronger local effect. R406 approaches acidic D1533 in 29.0% of vanilla models and
8.8% of masked models, and D1528 in 20.6% and 25.5%, respectively. These contacts are absent or rare
for WT G406 and are chemically plausible candidate electrostatic interactions. Masking redistributes
the arginine between the two acidic partners: it reduces R406-D1533 engagement while modestly
increasing R406-D1528 engagement.

R406-T1531 is nominally the most frequent nearest pair, occurring within 4 Å in essentially every
G406R model. It cannot yet be treated as a real interaction. Its median shortest distance is 2.3 Å in
vanilla and 1.3 Å when masked; 25.2% and 78.1% of models, respectively, contain distances below 2 Å.
These values indicate severe atomic overlap rather than a credible noncovalent contact. The overlap is
especially strong under masking and may contribute to apparent local shifts in G406R. Structural
inspection and side-chain relaxation are required before interpreting this pair or using the G406R
shortest-distance ensemble mechanistically.

Integrated preliminary interpretation

Taken together, the global, VSD, gate, and mutation-site analyses favor a local-repacking model for
both Cav1.2 mutants. G402S and G406R already alter their immediate S6-interface geometry in vanilla
predictions, and those directions are generally retained under masking. Masking mainly changes the
width and partner preference of the local ensembles rather than creating a new mutant state or a
consistent bias toward a more open pore. G402S has the weaker effect and exchanges a WT G402-M1524
proximity for an intermittent S402-I1523 contact. G406R has the stronger and more chemically specific
effect, sampling alternative acidic partners D1528 and D1533, but its especially short R406-T1531
distances reveal a concurrent steric-quality problem. The most defensible current conclusion is
therefore that targeted masking exposes additional local solutions around the mutant sites while
largely preserving the median global channel geometry. The G406R contact mechanism should remain
provisional until relaxation or structural inspection removes models with atomic overlap.

RMSF redistribution relative to the targeted masks

These values were recomputed from the final allOK3 ensembles and supersede the legacy all-model RMSF
profiles for quantitative interpretation. The analysis includes 39,986 accepted model snapshots:
4,995 G402S masked, 5,000 G402S vanilla, 4,997 G406R masked, 5,000 G406R vanilla, 4,998 G490R masked,
5,000 G490R vanilla, 4,996 WT masked, and 5,000 WT vanilla.

The filtered result supports a small, localized redistribution rather than broad destabilization.
Median masked-minus-vanilla RMSF within directly masked residues is +0.054 Å for G402S, +0.021 Å for
G406R, +0.042 Å for G490R, and +0.028 Å for WT. Outside the direct masks, the corresponding medians
are -0.003, -0.009, -0.015, and -0.021 Å. Only 47.3% of G490R and 48.1% of G406R residues increase,
compared with 51.3% for G402S and 44.0% for WT. Masking therefore does not increase RMSF uniformly
across CaV1.2; it slightly releases the targeted regions while maintaining or mildly reducing
variability through much of the remaining channel.

The provisional topology summaries also contain opposing regional effects. For example, WT DI S4
increases by approximately +0.73 Å and DII S1 by +0.11 Å, whereas DIII S1 decreases by approximately
-0.13 Å and the proposed DIV S4 interval by -2.33 Å. These annotations are educated sequence-based
boundaries and should be checked before residue-level mechanistic claims. The important robust result
is the coexistence of local gains and distal losses, which is more consistent with redistribution of
ensemble variability than with global expansion.

Because this pattern is present in WT as well as the mutants, it contains a substantial
protocol-dependent component. Mutation-specific interpretation should therefore rely on the
mutation-centered contact, distance, and reference-RMSD analyses rather than RMSF magnitude alone.
G490R remains best treated as a supplemental exploratory condition until its structural states are
supported by clustering and independent experimental comparison.
