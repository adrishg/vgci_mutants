## Paragraph P0004

Original: Many disease-associated mutations in voltage-gated ion channels are expected to alter gating through relatively small, state-dependent structural changes. These changes are difficult to capture experimentally, and default AlphaFold2 predictions are often nearly identical for a wild-type channel and its point mutant. Here, we hypothesized that this similarity arises in part from evolutionary constraints encoded in their largely shared multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) and studied whether targeted MSA masking could broaden mutation-relevant structural sampling. We generated AlphaFold2 structural ensembles spanning both sequence and MSA-processing conditions, comparing wild-type and mutant channels under the default (“vanilla”) and targeted-masking protocols. We evaluated these comparisons in three channel-specific case studies spanning mechanistic benchmark mutants and disease-associated variants: voltage-gated potassium Kv2.1 (L403A and F412L in the S6/pore-coupling region), voltage-gated sodium Nav1.5 (the QQQ mutant in the fast-inactivation gate), and voltage-gated calcium Cav1.2 (G402S and G406R at the DI-S6/DIV-S6 interface). We evaluated structural variability by channel, mutation, and masking strategy. In Kv2.1, masking increased access to the experimentally observed direction of the L403A displacement at the pore–VSD interface and generated contact-specific alternative packing arrangements around the unresolved F412L mutation. In Nav1.5, masking disrupted the wild-type IFM–receptor proximity supported by experimental structures, demonstrating that weakening evolutionary constraints can remove useful structural information. In Cav1.2, G402S altered local DI-S6/DIV-S6 packing and showed protocol-dependent redistribution of local partner selection, whereas unrelaxed G406R predictions included heterogeneous mutation-centered geometries and a substantial mutation-centered severe-overlap subset; neither mutation produced a uniform global pore shift. Overall, masking reshaped mutation-relevant local sampling in a channel- and region-dependent manner, increasing access to local alternatives in some cases while disrupting wild-type structural constraints in others. Together, these results support the use of targeted MSA masking as a context-dependent, hypothesis-generating strategy for exploring mutation-associated conformational alternatives, while emphasizing that sampled structures must be evaluated against channel-specific experimental data, mechanistic coordinates, and steric quality controls rather than interpreted as unique mutant conformations.

Revised: Disease-associated voltage-gated ion-channel mutations can alter state-dependent local structure while default AlphaFold2 predictions remain close to wild type. We tested targeted multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) masking in wild-type and mutant Kv2.1, Nav1.5, and Cav1.2 ensembles. Analyses reduced recycles within model-seed trajectories, weighted available AlphaFold2 models equally within recorded seed labels, and separated geometry among quality-qualified outputs from nominal-denominator retention and usable target yield. Masking redistributed rather than universally broadened predicted geometries. In Kv2.1 L403A, masking increased the maximum E423–N179 Cα distance by 1.1 Å and increased sampling beyond a prediction-independent, experiment-anchored cutoff by 12.3 percentage points, but it did not reproduce the coordinated 8SDA rearrangement. A recorded-label overlap interaction sensitivity was positive, but equality of actual random seeds was not independently verified and therefore did not establish a mutation-specific masking response. Kv2.1 masking also reduced analysis-final trajectory retention and redistributed F412L contacts without a mutation-matched structure. In Nav1.5, masking shifted wild-type predictions away from the experimentally supported IFM–receptor arrangement; complete IFM disengagement was not treated as an open-state marker. Atom-matched Cav1.2 G402S Cα analysis supported a small local packing change but not the earlier side-chain nearest-partner claim. G406R masking strongly reduced R406-centered local-overlap pass yield. These prediction frequencies generate structural hypotheses; they do not establish equilibrium occupancy, biological states, or unique mutant structures.

## Paragraph P0030

Thus, the principal experimental design varied two factors: sequence background (wild type versus mutant) and MSA treatment (vanilla versus masked). Wherever matched masks were available, this yielded four directly interpretable conditions—WT vanilla, WT masked, mutant vanilla, and mutant masked—allowing us to distinguish mutation-associated differences under the same MSA treatment from masking-dependent changes within the same sequence background. The shared Kv2.1 mask permitted direct sequence-by-masking interaction contrasts. In Nav1.5 and Cav1.2, WT–mutant vanilla comparisons were treated as the primary sequence contrasts, whereas masked WT–mutant comparisons were treated as protocol-specific explorations because the masked columns were not established to be identical between sequence backgrounds. ## Paragraph P0151

## Paragraph P0161

Original: Several limitations affect the interpretation of these ensembles. Recycle snapshots and subunits within a predicted structure are correlated. Primary uncertainty therefore resampled complete input seeds, retained the five AlphaFold2 model parameterizations as equal-weight within-seed strata, and reduced recycles within each seed-model trajectory before condition comparison. These intervals quantify prediction-run variability under a fixed modeling protocol and input alignment rather than biological or thermodynamic uncertainty. Representative models should therefore be viewed as illustrations of ensemble-supported hypotheses rather than unique mutant conformations.
