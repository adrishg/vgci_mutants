Kv2.1 preliminary results

Kv2.1 provides the clearest evidence that masking broadens the predicted structural ensemble. The
median masked-to-vanilla standard-deviation ratio is 1.42 for WT, 1.69 for L403A, and 1.73 for F412L
after trajectory-level pore–VSD interface QC. Approximately 94–95% of shared distances become more
variable. Median interquartile ranges increase by factors of 3.18, 2.30, and 3.00, respectively.

Broadening is strongest in S6, the intracellular gate, and the voltage sensors. The S6 profile shows
median standard-deviation increases of 3.50-fold for WT, 2.08-fold for L403A, and 1.92-fold for F412L.
The selectivity filter changes less, indicating that the result is not dominated by gross tetramer
disruption after structural quality control.

WT S6 geometry remains close to experimental WT structure 8SD3. L403A becomes more heterogeneous but
does not reproduce the contracted median S6 profile observed in 8SDA. Masking therefore increases
access to alternative geometries, but broader sampling and recovery of a mutation-specific experimental
state remain separate outcomes.

Asymmetric chain-C pore–VSD shift

A focused analysis of the chain-C S6-side pore–VSD interface reveals a directional response that is
obscured in the complete 24-distance panel. For E423-N179, the L403A median increases from 10.17 Å in
vanilla to 11.07 Å under masking, toward the 14.17 Å distance in experimental L403A structure 8SDA.
Vanilla therefore remains near the lower-distance geometry and does not capture the experimental
chain-C displacement, whereas masking samples in the correct direction.

The response is not limited to this single pair. All six chain-C coordinates connecting K427, E423,
or K420 to N179 or V182 move toward their corresponding 8SDA values under masking. The masked-minus-
vanilla shifts range from +0.53 to +1.75 Å: K427-N179 changes by +1.06 Å, K427-V182 by +1.75 Å,
E423-N179 by +0.90 Å, E423-V182 by +1.02 Å, K420-N179 by +0.53 Å, and K420-V182 by +0.60 Å.
These shifts recover only part of the experimental displacement, so the result supports enhanced
sampling toward an L403A-like asymmetric interface rather than full reproduction of 8SDA.

The experimental displacement is chain dependent: it is small in chains A and B, larger in chain C,
and largest in chain D. The predictions remain much more symmetric across A-D, even after masking.
Pooling chains into one distribution would therefore dilute the biologically relevant asymmetry and
treat correlated subunits from the same model as independent samples. The paper-facing figure should
retain chain C as the focused six-coordinate panel and show E423-N179 across all four chains as a
compact context panel. This displays the strongest reproducible direction while making its subunit
specificity and incomplete experimental recovery transparent.

Gate openness and coordinated state sampling

L403A is biased toward a slightly larger intracellular-gate span than protocol-matched WT in both
vanilla (+0.27 Å) and masked (+0.23 Å) ensembles. Its distal-S6 V409 span is also larger in both
conditions (+0.22 Å in vanilla and +0.29 Å when masked). Gate and distal-S6 geometry remain positively
correlated within L403A (Spearman rho = 0.51 in vanilla and 0.35 when masked), supporting a coordinated
shift rather than independent broadening. The direction is present without masking, while masking
mainly expands the range of available geometries. This does not mean that the ensemble reproduces the
8SDA L403A experimental state, whose contracted S6 profile remains distinct from the predicted median.

F412L shows the opposite median bias. Its gate is smaller than protocol-matched WT by 0.66 Å in
vanilla and 0.83 Å when masked, while the distal-S6 V409 span is lower by 0.60 Å and 0.96 Å,
respectively. The contracted bias therefore occurs in both protocols and becomes stronger with
masking. However, model-by-model gate-S6 coordination is weak in vanilla F412L (rho = 0.16) and weakly
negative when masked (rho = -0.20). Masked F412L samples a much wider range, but that extra variability
does not follow a single coherent opening or closing coordinate.

The two mutants are therefore state-biased in different directions in both vanilla and masked
predictions. L403A shows the clearer coordinated gate-S6 displacement, whereas F412L shows the larger
mask-dependent expansion without equivalent internal coordination.

Voltage-sensor bias relative to WT

F412L is the only mutant in the present set with a consistent shift toward shorter VSD landmark
distances. Both shared chain-A contacts are shorter than WT in both protocols. F236-R289 changes by
-0.02 Å in vanilla and -0.03 Å when masked, while F236-R308 changes by -0.13 Å and -0.09 Å,
respectively. The direction is therefore already present in vanilla and is retained, rather than
created or strongly amplified, by masking. The magnitude is small, so this supports a subtle VSD
contraction rather than assignment to a distinct voltage-sensor state.

L403A does not show the same behavior. In vanilla, F236-R289 is shorter by 0.05 Å but F236-R308 is
longer by 0.01 Å. Under masking both contacts are longer by approximately 0.03 Å. These small mixed
changes provide no evidence for a uniform L403A VSD contraction.

Only chain A is used for this cross-protocol test because the WT vanilla CSV does not provide the
corresponding four-chain VSD block. The conclusion is therefore limited to the two shared F236-R289
and F236-R308 landmarks and should not be generalized to all four voltage sensors without additional
matched measurements.

Mutation-side-chain contact analysis

L403A does not form a recurrent new mutation-side-chain contact in the current structurally filtered
ensemble. No tested alanine-partner pair exceeds a 4.3% contact fraction, and most are below 1%.
Several contacts made by WT L403 are instead lost after substitution by the smaller alanine side
chain. The clearest example is the paper-numbered L403A-L316B pair in vanilla predictions: the WT
contact fraction is 76.9%, whereas the L403A ensemble has no models at or below 4 Å. Under masking,
the corresponding WT L403 contacts to L316B and L316D are also common, but fall to 0.6% and 1.1% in
L403A. The local effect is therefore better described as removal and loosening of hydrophobic packing
than formation of a new stabilizing interaction.

F412L produces more extensive S6-neighborhood repacking. The paper-numbered L412-L403D contact occurs
in 42.4% of vanilla F412L models, and L412-I407D occurs in 17.6%. Under masking, L412-L403B and
L412-I407B contacts occur in 35.4% and 13.4% of models, respectively; their median distances are
8.8 Å and 7.6 Å shorter than the protocol-matched WT Phe distances. The preferred chain partner
therefore changes between protocols, consistent with the broader and weakly coordinated masked F412L
ensemble rather than a single rigid rearrangement. A small fraction of masked L412-L403B models
(2.8%) has distances below 2 Å, so this contact population requires steric inspection before being
assigned mechanistic significance.

The contact analysis reinforces the distinction between the mutants. L403A primarily removes local
side-chain packing while retaining a modest coordinated gate-S6 displacement. F412L creates several
alternative close-packing arrangements and shows the stronger contracted gate/S6 bias, but masking
distributes that response across alternative partners and introduces a minor overlap population.
Neither result by itself demonstrates recovery of the experimental 8SDA state.

Hydrophobic coupling nexus as a targeted structural benchmark

The F412-centered hydrophobic nexus provides a compact mechanistic test of model quality. The current
analysis uses three shortest heavy-atom coordinates: F412 to L316 in the same subunit, and the minimum
F412-to-L329 and F412-to-L403 distances across neighboring subunits. For F412L predictions, the
corresponding L412 distances are used. This three-coordinate representation is compared with 8SD3 as
the mutation-matched WT reference. Because no mutation-matched F412L experimental structure is
included, 8SDA is used only to test sampling of an L403A-like nexus state, not absolute F412L accuracy.

In 8SD3, the three shortest nexus distances are 3.89 Å for F412-L316, 3.82 Å for F412-L329, and
3.77 Å for F412-L403. Relative to this vector, masking improves the WT ensemble: the median
three-coordinate root-mean-square distance to 8SD3 decreases from 1.57 Å in vanilla to 1.37 Å when
masked. The fraction of WT models within 1.5 Å of the experimental nexus increases from 32.0% to
76.5%. Thus, targeted masking improves local WT nexus agreement even though it broadens other channel
coordinates.

The F412L result is different. Relative to the 8SDA state-reference vector, the median nexus error is
1.43 Å in vanilla and 1.51 Å when masked, and the fraction within 1.5 Å decreases from 66.0% to 49.1%.
Masking therefore does not move the center of the F412L ensemble closer to 8SDA. It does, however,
create a closer tail: 3.3% of masked models fall within 1.0 Å of the 8SDA nexus, compared with 0.04%
of vanilla models. This supports the narrower claim that masking samples an additional
experiment-like nexus subpopulation, not that the complete masked F412L ensemble is more accurate.

The apparent gain requires steric qualification. Approximately 3.5% of masked F412L models contain a
sub-2 Å L412-L403 shortest distance, whereas the corresponding vanilla nexus has no such overlap.
Nexus fidelity should therefore be reported both before and after removing or relaxing clashing
models. A convincing masking benefit would require enrichment of the low-error population after this
steric filter and coordination of the nexus score with the experimentally directed S6/gate shift.

Exploratory F412L-specific nexus states

The absence of a solved F412L structure makes the internal organization of its predicted ensemble
particularly informative. F412L does not simply weaken all three nexus contacts. Instead, it
redistributes packing among them. In vanilla, the median L412-L316, L412-L329, and L412-L403 shortest
distances are 5.0, 6.3, and 4.0 Å, respectively. Relative to the 8SD3 WT nexus, F412L therefore loses
the close L329 interaction while forming a tighter and much more frequent L412-L403 contact. This
contact redistribution offers a structural explanation for how replacing the aromatic F412 side
chain with leucine could alter coupling without globally unfolding the S6 region.

The vanilla ensemble is dominated by one neighboring-subunit orientation. The closest L329 and L403
partners are both in chain D for 78.8% of models and both in chain B for 19.3%; chain C is selected in
only 1.9%. Masking changes this balance rather than producing a uniform expansion. Both contacts are
chain-B-centered in 52.0% of models and chain-D-centered in 41.5%. The two partner identities agree
in 94.3% of masked models and 100% of vanilla models. This concordance indicates that these are
alternative orientations of the coupled nexus, rather than independent fluctuations of individual
side chains. Because Kv2.1 is homotetrameric, chain-B versus chain-D occupancy may also represent
symmetry-related clockwise and counterclockwise solutions. Structural alignment with a fixed chain-A
reference is required before treating them as functionally distinct substates.

Masking also makes these orientations structurally more heterogeneous. The L412-L329 interquartile
range expands from 6.1-6.7 Å in vanilla to 6.2-9.3 Å when masked, while the L412-L403 lower tail
extends to 2.2 Å at the tenth percentile. The latter includes the 3.5% steric-overlap population, but
the experiment-like tail is not eliminated by removing those models: after excluding every nexus
distance below 2 Å, 3.4% of masked F412L models remain within 1.0 Å of the 8SDA three-contact nexus,
compared with 0.04% of vanilla models. Masking therefore reveals a genuine low-error subpopulation
alongside a separate overpacked population that requires relaxation.

The nexus alternatives are coupled to broader S6 geometry. In masked F412L, the L412-L403 distance
correlates negatively with intracellular-gate span (Spearman rho = -0.61) and positively with the
distal-S6 V409 span (rho = +0.73). The ten percent of masked models closest to the 8SDA nexus have a
median gate span of 12.91 Å and V409 span of 16.30 Å, compared with 13.45 and 16.53 Å for the complete
masked ensemble. The corresponding vanilla low-error subset is only slightly contracted. Thus, the
masked experiment-like nexus population is associated with a modestly more contracted pore/S6
geometry rather than being an isolated side-chain rearrangement.

These results support a testable structural hypothesis for the unresolved mutant. F412L may populate
at least two neighboring-subunit coupling orientations, with masking increasing exchange between
chain-B- and chain-D-centered packing. A minority substate combines an 8SDA-like loosened L329
contact, reconfigured L403 packing, and a contracted intracellular S6 geometry. This should be
presented as an F412L-specific predicted substate—not as a reconstructed experimental structure—and
prioritized for side-chain relaxation, clustering, and comparison with the mutation's functional
inactivation phenotype.

Corrected experimental-reference RMSD analysis

The corrected v2 RMSD calculation resolves the earlier approximately 31 Å artifact. The analysis
uses only structures in the allOK3 convergence manifest, excludes rows flagged by the v2 chain-mapping
QC, applies the same tetramer/interface allowlists used by the distance notebooks, and removes complete
trajectories in the separated alignment-failure population. The latter begins at a stable-core RMSD of
5.77 Å, after an empty 4.0–5.7 Å interval, and simultaneously shows 10.9–16 Å S6-bundle RMSD. A
conservative 4 Å stable-core cutoff is therefore used as a topology/alignment failure threshold rather
than as a trimming rule for the continuous conformational distribution. This retains 24,269 unique
model snapshots: 4,562 WT vanilla, 3,658 WT masked, 4,371 L403A vanilla, 3,693 L403A masked, 4,407
F412L vanilla, and 3,578 F412L masked. The mapping-QC filter removes 1,245 allOK3 models, and the
subsequent structural filter removes 388 additional models.

Paired reference resemblance was calculated as RMSD(8SDA)-RMSD(8SD3), where negative values indicate
greater resemblance to the experimental L403A structure and positive values indicate greater
resemblance to WT 8SD3. At the whole-tetramer level, F412L provides the clearest mutant-specific
result. The median paired score is -0.297 Å in vanilla and -0.215 Å under masking; 90.2% and 85.1% of
the corresponding models, respectively, are closer to 8SDA than to 8SD3. Thus, F412L has a robust
global bias toward the L403A-like experimental arrangement even though no F412L structure was
available for fitting or validation.

The F412L resemblance is not a uniform copy of 8SDA. In the four-chain S6 bundle, the fraction closer
to 8SDA is 33.9% in vanilla and 48.2% under masking. In distal S6 it is 1.9% and 31.6%, and in the
hydrophobic coupling nexus it is 3.0% and 31.4%. Masking therefore shifts substantial F412L
subpopulations toward the L403A experimental geometry in the mechanistically relevant S6 and nexus
regions, while the medians remain on or near the WT side of the reference boundary. The most
defensible interpretation is that F412L favors a globally L403A-related state and that masking
increases access to locally L403A-like S6/nexus substates; it does not reproduce every local feature
of 8SDA.

WT and L403A remain predominantly closer to 8SD3 in the localized S6 and nexus RMSDs. Masking broadens
both ensembles and increases their 8SDA-like tails. For example, the masked fraction closer to 8SDA
in the four-chain S6 bundle is 20.6% for WT and 13.5% for L403A, compared with 1.6% and 2.1% in
vanilla. The apparently weak L403A reference preference is not necessarily inconsistent with the
experimentally directed E423-N179 distance shift. RMSD averages many atomic displacements and can
dilute a small, directional, chain-specific movement. The chain-specific distance panel and the
regional RMSD distributions should therefore be presented together.

Across all sequences, masking raises median absolute RMSD to each individual reference, consistent
with broader sampling rather than uniformly improved static accuracy. The relevant benefit is
redistribution and access to alternative experimental-like subpopulations. This distinction is most
important for the unresolved F412L mutant: vanilla already shows a strong whole-channel 8SDA bias,
whereas masking preferentially increases the local S6 and nexus fractions that cross toward 8SDA.

RMSF redistribution relative to the targeted masks

These values were recomputed from the final allOK3 structural/interface-QC ensembles and supersede
the legacy all-model RMSF profiles for quantitative interpretation. The analysis includes 25,191
accepted model snapshots: 3,841 F412L masked, 4,521 F412L vanilla, 3,910 L403A masked, 4,403 L403A
vanilla, 3,925 WT masked, and 4,591 WT vanilla. Kv2.1 is reported both by chain and as a
symmetry-averaged tetramer profile.

The filtered result sharpens the masking interpretation. Median masked-minus-vanilla RMSF within
directly masked residues is +1.47 Å for WT, +1.31 Å for L403A, and +1.31 Å for F412L. Outside the
direct masks, however, the median changes are -0.24, -0.40, and -0.47 Å, respectively. The fractions
of all residues with increased RMSF are only 54.8%, 54.5%, and 54.0%. Masking therefore does not
produce a uniform global broadening. It strongly releases the targeted pore/VSD-coupling region while
reducing median variability across much of the rest of the protein.

The largest provisional segment-level increases lie in the S4-S5 linker, S5, pore helix/selectivity
filter region, and S6. In WT, their median changes are approximately +5.03, +6.52, +4.85, and
+2.31 Å; L403A gives +4.47, +5.87, +4.32, and +1.91 Å; F412L gives +4.86, +6.52, +4.99, and
+1.30 Å. These boundaries are sequence-based annotations and should be checked before detailed
residue-level claims, but the spatial concentration of the effect is robust.

This result supports targeted conformational release rather than nonspecific destabilization. It also
means that the paper should not describe masking as globally increasing Kv2.1 RMSF. The defensible
claim is that masking redistributes sampling toward the targeted electromechanical-coupling and pore
segments, while the distance and experimental-reference analyses determine whether the newly sampled
substates are mechanistically or experimentally relevant.
